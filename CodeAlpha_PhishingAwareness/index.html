THE BASICS 01 / 04

A familiar message.
A hidden motive.

Phishing is a deceptive message designed to make you reveal information, open a harmful file, send money, or visit a fake website.

It can arrive by email, text, phone call, social media, or collaboration apps. Attackers often impersonate a trusted brand or person and create pressure so you act before checking.

01
⌛

Unexpected urgency

“Act now” or “your account closes today” is meant to rush your judgment.

02
◎

Odd sender or link

Look for misspellings, extra words, or a domain that is almost—but not quite—right.

03
▤

Unusual request

Be wary of unexpected requests for passwords, payment, gift cards, or confidential files.

THREE THINGS TO QUESTION

01

Sender doesn't match
Compare the full domain with the organization's official domain.

02

Pressure and threat
Pause. Contact the organization through a known channel.

03

Password request by link
Never enter credentials from an unexpected message link.

A single clue is a reason to verify. Several together? Don't interact.
ALSO WATCH FOR

More than email.

SMS / SMISHING

A delivery text asks you to pay a small “redelivery fee” through a shortened link.

VOICE / VISHING

A caller claiming to be your bank asks you to read out a one-time login code.

IMPERSONATION

A “manager” messages urgently asking you to buy gift cards and send the codes.

A SAFER RESPONSE 03 / 04

Make a small pause
your strongest defense.

You don't need to prove a message is malicious. You only need to verify before taking a risky action.

01

Stop

Don't click, reply, open an attachment, scan a QR code, or share a verification code.

⏸
02

Check independently

Open the official app or type a known address yourself. Contact the sender using a number or address you already trust.

⌕
03

Report and remove

Use your organization's phishing-report option or contact its IT/security team. Then delete the message.

↗
04

If you already interacted

Tell IT/security promptly. Change a disclosed password from the official site, revoke active sessions if available, and contact your bank if payment details were shared.

!
THE GOLDEN RULE

Never share your password or one-time code because a message asks you to.

✳

Build habits that help

  • Use unique passwords and a password manager.
  • Turn on multi-factor authentication.
  • Keep devices and apps updated.
  • Verify payment or account changes through a second channel.
